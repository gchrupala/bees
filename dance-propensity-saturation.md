# Why evolved `dance_propensity` comes out flat (~0.6)

In the disk food-distribution matrix (branch `food-site-geometry`, run 2026-07-04), the evolved `dance_propensity` trait came out nearly flat at ~0.58–0.72 across the whole count×radius grid, with only a faint bump near 150 m radius — it did not develop the spatial structure the other traits (directional bias, recruitment advantage) show. The cause is the functional form: a successful scout dances with probability `P = 1 − (1 − s)^remaining_capacity`, which saturates to ≈1 for any modest `s` once `remaining_capacity` exceeds ~1 (e.g. s=0.6, remaining=6 → P=0.996). With area-scaled capacity, most patches at 150 m+ carry capacity ≥6, so `remaining` is almost always >1 and essentially every `s` value yields P≈1. The trait therefore only feels selection at capacity-1 (tiny or near-depleted) patches; everywhere else it is near-neutral and drifts to a middling value.

To make it feel selection, try a less-saturating map from trait to dance probability — e.g. linear `P = min(1, s · remaining)` with a small per-load slope, or a logistic in remaining capacity with an evolvable threshold, or keep the geometric form but shrink base capacity / the capacity reference radius so remaining capacities stay small. See the surrounding disk food-site model design (geometry, gamma forays, area capacity, Poisson counts) for context.

## Fitness-gradient probe (2026-07-04)

Ran a fixed-`s` sweep — froze each cell at its evolved operating point and varied `dance_propensity` 0→1 without evolution, measuring colony payoff. Key result: the payoff-vs-s gradient is NEVER peaked at an intermediate value. It is flat for s≥0.1 at large capacity (600 m radius) and in degenerate tiny-patch cells (payoff floored at ~0), and monotone-INCREASING (saturating near s≈0.9–1.0) at low-per-patch/dense cells (8×75 m) and moderately at 150 m. So both failure modes coexist: genuine flat selection (→ drift to ~0.6) where capacity saturates, and real-but-under-resolved upward selection (true optimum ≈1.0, GA stalls at ~0.7) where a gradient exists.

Crucially, NO cell favors low s — the "suppress recruitment where it doesn't pay" biology has no purchase because dancing is nearly free (`base_dance_cost=0`; only `cue_cost=0.02·bias` and `attention_cost=0.01`/follow). Therefore reshaping the trait→probability map is necessary but NOT sufficient: without a real cost of wasted recruitment (nonzero `base_dance_cost`, or wasted recruits' travel cost counting against the colony) a non-saturating map just evolves s→1, not an informative interior optimum. **Fix = non-saturating map AND a dancing/wasted-recruit cost**; then re-run this probe to confirm an interior peak that varies across the grid.

## Cost quantification (component breakdown)

One dance costs `cue_cost·bias` ≈ 0.012–0.017 (`base_dance_cost=0`), <2% of the value (`food_value=1.0`) of the single recruited success it can produce; total dance cost is ~1% of gross food per episode (0.9–1.6% across cells) and attention adds ~1–2%. Travel cost dominates (−0.38 to −1.0/episode, 100–500× the dance cost). A recruit arriving at a depleted patch just fails at ~the same cost as the random solo search it would otherwise have done, so there is no payoff term penalizing wasted recruitment beyond the ~0.014 cue cost.

Also note: `evaluate_colony` floors per-colony payoff at `max(0.001, …)`; at moderate/dense cells (150 m, 8×75 m) the raw net is NEGATIVE (travel > food), so payoff there is mostly the floor plus a few lucky profitable colonies — the only robustly-profitable probed cell (c2_r600 m, net +2.1) is exactly where the s-gradient is dead flat, strengthening the no-selection conclusion.
